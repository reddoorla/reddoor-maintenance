## How the rule moved, and what each fix broke

- **Round 2: `:focus-within` lost every mouse click.** The still layout was keyed on `:focus-within`. Chromium focuses a link on mousedown, so the row reflowed into a column under the pointer, the mouseup landed elsewhere, and no click fired. Only link 1, which stayed under the pointer, worked. Re-keyed on `:has(:focus-visible)`.
- **Round 3: a key after a mouse focus.** Chromium makes a mouse-focused link match `:focus-visible` as soon as a key goes down. Shift, the arrows, Escape and letters all do it. Then the window grew from 33px to 113px at 1280px, and `/s/longname` under the pointer became `/s/acme`. Fixed with a `data-pointer` flag that only Tab cleared.
- **Round 4: the flag was a guess.** M1: a right press, Escape, then the pointer leaving let the ringed link scroll away 13 s later. M2: a click inside the still list moved focus without `:focus-visible`, so the row snapped back and link 2 jumped from (94, 478) to (938, 438). The fix made the still layout a state the component holds (`data-still`). It comes on for a focus from outside that was not a press, or at a re-check. It goes off only when focus leaves the window. `data-js` gives a no-script fallback.
- **Round 5: touch.** A tap's events arrive as `pointerup` and then `focusin`, so "pressing" was already false and taps on links 2 and 3 were lost. Fixed with a 1000 ms tap window, a re-check on mount (a focus made before hydration was lost), `contextmenu` and `blur` clearing a stuck press, and a held layout during a press on non-link text.
- **Round 6: the tap window was page-wide.** A tap anywhere, then Tab within 1 s, left the row moving: link 1 went from x −42 to −493 over 8 s. The window now counts only presses that began in the ticker, plus a re-check at 1050 ms. The fixer then found that Chromium leaves `:hover` on a tapped element, so the re-check never fired. The addendum, A.4, used a page-wide "last pointer type".
- **Round 7: A.4 was wrong.** A resting mouse fires no `pointerover`, so after any touch the hover rule was skipped. A swipe, then Shift, stilled the row under the mouse (link 1 jumped from x 74 to 94). Replaced with `finePointerOver`, set only by the ticker window's own mouse or pen events. `:hover` is no longer read.
- **Round 8: a drag is a leave.** At `dragstart` Chromium fires `pointercancel` and then the window's `pointerleave`, at 0,0, with the mouse still over it, as the Pointer Events spec requires. A click that slipped 12px into a drag, then a key, stilled the row under the mouse: 8 of 10 runs at 1280px, 5 of 10 at 390px. The fix was a `dragend` that settles the flag from the drop point.
- **Round 9: no second leave.** After an inside drop, the first move out of the row sends the window no `pointerleave`. Chromium thinks the mouse already left. The ringed link slid out in 60 of 60 runs. The fix: `dragend` arms a one-shot page listener, and the next mouse or pen event anywhere decides.
- **Round 10: off the page, no pointer event at all.** With the row at the viewport's bottom, a drop 1px inside, then a 3px move off the browser, failed 30 of 30. The operator asked for a measurement first. 285 logged runs showed that Chromium's mouse-event boundary tracking survives a drag and only the pointer tracking resets. A document `mouseout` with `relatedTarget === null` fired 180 of 180 times. Pointer events fired 61 of 180 times, depending on the rest (1 of 60 at 0 ms, 41 of 60 at 150 ms). Clearing the flag on that `mouseout` fixed it: 90 of 90 against 2 of 90 at the control.

Each fix from round 6 on was narrow and correct for what it targeted. Each one moved the rule onto a signal Chromium tracks differently from the last. The decision files said so from round 9: "each extra round has so far found a narrower edge of the same pointer-tracking problem". Every drag was driven through Playwright's intercepted drag, never an OS drag. Real Chrome's timing after a real drop is unverified, and a touch drag (Windows, ChromeOS) could not be reproduced on Linux at all ([reddoorla/reddoor-maintenance#2135](https://github.com/reddoorla/reddoor-maintenance/issues/2135)).

## Beliefs corrected on contact

- **"Polling `animation-play-state` fixes the pause race"** (`9e890ce2`). It does not. The computed property flips at once, and the pause settles a frame later: the reads were −21.0992px, then −22.0165px. The fix waits on `getAnimations()[0].playState` and then two frames. Mutant 35, which removes that wait, still goes red on the race more often than not ([reddoorla/reddoor-maintenance#2124](https://github.com/reddoorla/reddoor-maintenance/issues/2124)).
- **Chromium was said to report `dragend` at 0,0 in some cases.** It did so in 0 of 1021 logged drags. The guard stays only as a safety, and Firefox reports (0, −86), which the guard misses ([reddoorla/reddoor-maintenance#2136](https://github.com/reddoorla/reddoor-maintenance/issues/2136)).
- **Round 8's Result said the stale hover after an outside drop comes "about 400 ms" later.** The tests lens logged 6 to 170 ms.
- **The drag-out test's comment** said the 3px move makes the hover "the real one by the key press". In 7 of 10 runs the stale `pointerover` came after the move, and the test passed only because the key landed first, with 17 ms margins. Round 10 then found there was no race at all: the late `pointerover` comes 16 to 51 ms after the row goes still, because the still layout grows the window under the pointer.
- **"The list overflows the window and can be hit just below it."** The window is `overflow: hidden`, and its hit area ends at its box: 0.5px below is already outside at every width.
- **Mutants 79 and 83 "red" at the bar of 8** were the stale-hover flake, not kills. At the bar of 5 both survive. Round 11 tried 58 times between two lenses and could not kill either.
- **Round 11's F2:** the round-8 pen-leave test flakes, 4 of 18 runs on the fix and 2 of 18 on `974df34a`. In one mutation run it made the equivalent mutant 83 look red. A survivor that goes red only on that test is that flake ([reddoorla/reddoor-maintenance#2139](https://github.com/reddoorla/reddoor-maintenance/issues/2139)).

## Round 11's load lesson

The container has 4 CPUs. For the first half hour of round 11, three browser lenses and the PR 7b implementer ran at once. Load averages reached 80 to 115, tests crashed, and every count taken in that window was thrown away. Every count in round 11's decision was retaken one browser at a time, fix and control back to back. Round 7 had already seen one unexplained file-level failure of `snapshot-navigation` while lenses shared the machine ([reddoorla/reddoor-maintenance#2129](https://github.com/reddoorla/reddoor-maintenance/issues/2129)), and round 10 saw a bar of 8 fail once. Neither was traced to load at the time. The rule now in the brief: one browser-heavy job at a time on this container, and the full mutation run in `--only` chunks with nothing else beside it.
