# 2026-11-17 — P1-113: freeze the clocks the +90 time-travel run catches

Landed in this PR (`claude/p1-113-time-travel`), tests only. Closes the wall-clock half of #1978 when the next scheduled `time-travel` run on `main` goes green.

The weekly `time-travel` run (+90 days) went red on 11-09 (run 42700847910, one test) and 11-16 (run 43953299107, three tests). Reproduced here at `a4c15ef` under `REDDOOR_TIME_TRAVEL_DAYS=90`: the two TLS positive controls in `tests/audits/domain.test.ts` failed 3 of 3 runs (`expected 'warn' to be 'pass'`), and the streak test in `tests/cli/report-test-send.test.ts` failed 1 of 3 (`streak=1` that time, `streak=0` in CI). The guard test passed each time, so the shim had applied.

**The TLS controls** compared two different clocks. openssl mints the 30-day cert from the real clock, and the TLS handshake checks its expiry against the real clock, which the shim cannot touch. But the audit's `now` was `new Date()`, which the shim moves. At +90 the cert read 60 days past expiry, so `certDaysRemaining` was negative and the verdict was `warn`. `now` is now the cert's own `validFromDate` (its notBefore), so the cert has exactly 30 days left whatever the wall clock says.

**The streak test had no window to fix.** The brief guessed it counted over a time window; it does not. `cleanSendStreak` counts `clean` rows back from the newest, in `(sent_at, id)` order, and `sent_at` is the send's `new Date()`. The shim fakes `Date` with `shouldAdvanceTime`, and a probe showed that clock moving in 20 ms jumps (`.436, .436, .456, .456, .456, .456` against the real clock's 3 to 4 ms steps). Three sends in a row therefore shared one `sent_at`, and the random `ts_<uuid>` ids decided which counted as newest. That makes the failure a coin toss more than a 90-day decay: with the fix reverted it failed 6 of 10 runs. Each send now runs at its own frozen instant, a minute apart on 2026-11-16, through a small `atFrozenTime` helper. The helper puts back whichever clock was running: a probe test placed after it read `shift_days=90.000 fake=true` under the shim and `0.000 fake=false` without it. Production orders tied sends the same way, but only for sends inside one real millisecond, so it was left alone (no production change was in scope).

**Mutations** (each under +90): reverting the TLS `now` turned both controls red; minting the cert with `-days 7` (inside the 14-day warning window from the frozen now) turned both red; making the problem send the newest dropped the streak and turned the test red; reverting the streak fix turned it red 6 of 10 runs. The brief's mutation 3 said "move a timestamp outside the counted window". There is no window, so the nearest test of the same thing was used: reorder the timestamps.

**A belief corrected:** the brief expected `days=0` to be a valid dispatch. It is not: `vitest.time-travel-setup.ts` throws on any value that is not a positive number, by design. The real-clock half is the PR's own CI run, which runs the same suite without the shim.

Review round 1 (Opus) found four nits and nothing else. All four were folded in: the restore reuses `TIME_TRAVEL_FAKES`; the helper's comment says it is not re-entrant and that its sends sit at a fixed date rather than +90; and the TLS test uses `validFromDate`, not parsing of `validFrom` text. `tests/scripts/dropbox.test.ts` (the HTTPS_PROXY case) fails in this container on both clocks. That is the container's proxy, not this change; CI is the authority on it.
