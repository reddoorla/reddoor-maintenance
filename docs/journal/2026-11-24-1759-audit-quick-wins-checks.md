# 2026-11-24 — audit-review quick wins: robots, penalty words, today's date

Landed in reddoorla/reddoor-maintenance#2273 (`claude/qw-checks`). Three items from the 11-23 audit-system review's quick wins (workspace `audits/2026-11-23-audit-system-review/report.md` §4), worked across three sessions because the first two handed off at the 300k-token seam.

**SEO-03, the robots blanket block.** The check was one regex, `user-agent\s*:\s*\*[^]*?^\s*disallow\s*:\s*\/\s*$`, whose lazy `[^]*?` ran past the end of the `*` group. A stock hosted-builder robots.txt (`User-agent: *` / `Allow: /`, then a `User-agent: PetalBot` / `Disallow: /` group further down) failed a site that lets every crawler in. It now reads the `*` groups through `parseRobots` and decides on the patterns: a Disallow that matches every path (`/`, `/*`, `*`, `/*$`), lifted only by an Allow that also matches every path and is at least as long. The first two designs decided on a sample path, and review found what that costs: `Allow: /$` (the home page alone) lifted a whole-site block, and a second rule like `Disallow: /a` changed the verdict. `matchesEveryPath` reads the pattern itself, so no path decides it. Review round 3 also found that a robots.txt with CR-only line endings parsed as one line; `parseRobots` now splits on CR, LF or CRLF as RFC 9309 says. Round 5 ran about 30 files through `runSiteChecks` and every verdict matched the crawl's own `robotsAllowsUs`.

**PR-12, today's date.** A model-written fix had called a past date "ahead of today", because the analyze prompt carried no date. `buildAnalyzeInput` now takes an injectable `now` and states it. The date is UTC, so after 4pm PT it is tomorrow's; that is filed as #2278 rather than fixed here.

**B-09, penalty words: mostly not shipped.** The statute sentence "Penalties include civil suits … and fines ranging from $5,000" still passes `price-signal` as a price, as on `main`. Four review rounds in a row (3 to 6) found the word "fines" covering a real price in a new sense: herbs (fines herbes, with a hyphen, with an en dash, "Fines herbs"), a place name (Fines Creek), and on the sixth round the materials that landscape suppliers sell (crusher fines, screened fines, limestone fines). Narrowing to penalty frames ("fines of / ranging / up to / starting at") on the operator's round-5 pick did not help: the materials use exactly those frames. On his round-6 pick the bare word came out and only `fined` and `a fine of`, which no round faulted, ship. Every price those rounds produced is now a test that it stays a price. B-09 is #2276, with the direction the rounds point to: read a penalty off the sentence, not off one word.
