# 2026-11-23 — Code never upgrades an accuracy verdict to `confirmed` (this PR, `claude/b02-never-upgrade-to-confirmed`)

`verifyQuotes` had one rule that moved a verdict up. When the model called a claim `contradicted` and the site used any of that claim's distinctive search terms exactly, the verdict became `confirmed`, with an 80-character window around the term as the "site quote". It was written for our own site, which lists an out-of-state mailing address on one page and its metro area on another: the model called the metro claim contradicted, and the site does say both.

**What it did to a stored report.** On one stored prospect audit, two claims the model had called contradicted were printed under "checked and fine" as matching a passage on the business's own site. One said the business is a kind of business it is not. The other said no business operates under its exact name. The terms that did the flipping were the model's evidence *against* the claims: a phrase saying what the business actually does, and the business's own name in its footer. Finding them on the site was taken as the site agreeing. The window around the first then passed `quoteSupportsClaim`, because it happened to share two words with the claim. Sharing a term is not saying the thing.

**The rule now.** Code may only move a verdict down, and only where "your site does say this" is a reason to doubt the model. The term must be part of the claim's own wording, and the claim must not be a negation (a small `NEGATED` pattern: no, not, never, none, nobody, without, n't). Then the verdict becomes `unverified`, quotes the site around the term, and gives the reason `Your site does say "<term>", so we have not called this wrong.` Otherwise the model's verdict and its own quote stand, under the usual quote checks. Only the model can make a verdict `confirmed`.

**Review round 1 changed the rule.** The first version of this PR only swapped `confirmed` for `unverified`. The review showed that this still printed "your site does say <the model's counter-evidence>, so we have not called this wrong" on exactly the two claims that started this, and threw away the model's true contradicting quote. The same round found that the first fixture reused the stored site's sentences closely enough to search for. The fixtures are now invented words that reproduce the same mechanism.

**Tests.** The old test "flips contradicted to confirmed when the site uses the claim's own terms" pinned the behaviour this change removes. It now expects `unverified`, the site passage and the full reason. "keeps contradicted when the site never uses the claim's terms" asserted only "not confirmed"; it now also asserts that no downgrade reason was added. A new test holds a supported contradiction at `contradicted` with no reason. The two neutral fixtures came out `confirmed` on the old code; now they keep the model's quote, with no "your site does say". Mutations: the whole old `accuracy.ts`, 3 red; restoring `verdict = "confirmed"` in the downgrade, 1 red; dropping the term-in-claim guard, 1 red; dropping the negation guard, 1 red; dropping the reason, 1 red; downgrading every `contradicted`, 1 red.

**Stored reports.** The stored rows keep only the final verdict, so a past flip cannot be read off a row directly. It leaves a signature: a `confirmed` site quote that is exactly the ±80-character window around a term's first exact occurrence in the stored crawl's site text. A read-only sweep of all 74 rows (8 carry accuracy assertions, 39 of those assertions `confirmed` with a quote) found that signature on 7 assertions in 4 rows, ours among them. Under the new rule, 4 of the 7 would come down to `unverified` and 3 would stay with the model, whose original quote the row no longer holds. Changing them changes no headline and no fix on any of the 4 reports (`toReportView` → `headlineFinding` / `allFixes`, before and after); only the "checked and fine" list changes. The per-report table is in the workspace decision file, since the rows name prospects. This PR writes nothing to Turso.
