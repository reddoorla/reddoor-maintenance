# 2026-10-26 — P1-78d: the operator digest on the severity ladder

Landed in reddoorla/reddoor-maintenance (this PR). Brief: workspace `briefs/2026-10-26-p1-78d-digest-ladder.md`; plan: `plans/2026-10-26-cockpit-for-the-team/plan.md`, PR 3 of 8.

**Why.** The digest mailed one flat "Needs attention" list. Once P1-78b put the cockpit on Broken, Attention and Watch, that list would have mailed housekeeping (a day-one Renovate PR, a Prismic check that could not run) beside a site a visitor cannot reach. The digest now follows the ladder:

- Broken is mailed first, then Attention.
- Watch stays in `digest_state`, where its age lives, and is never mailed.
- "Act:" names only a Broken or Attention item.
- A rise in level is news.

**What changed.**

- **Ordering.** `orderAttention` ranks by level, then severity. A site keeps its items together and sorts by its worst.
- **Where Watch is dropped.** Watch leaves the email after `diffAttention`, so it keeps aging. A Renovate PR red for seven days reaches Attention on day 7, from an empty state, and is mailed then.
- **The send log.** It records the level each key was mailed at:
  - a skipped night keeps the level last mailed, as the metric keeps its mailed baseline;
  - a rise in level at the same count is "worse".
- **Badges.** They now come from the send log when it was read (`badgeFromSendLog`). A key first mailed after its Watch days is NEW. A key that rose since the last mail is WORSE, though the snapshot calls both standing.
- **"Act:".** It names a changed critical item or a changed Broken item, Broken first.

**Five review rounds**, three lenses each (correctness, tests and evidence, what the operator receives): 1 to 4 dirty, 5 clean.

- **Round 1:** the send log's level was not proven end to end, and a skipped night overwrote the level last mailed.
- **Round 2:** a Renovate PR first mailed on day 7 carried no NEW badge, because the snapshot had held it for a week. Keying that badge on `sentOn` broke two cases; it is now keyed on whether the send log was read.
- **Round 3:**
  - "Act:" picked by severity alone, so a new failed production deploy (Broken, warning) lost the subject to a new critical vuln (Attention).
  - The send-log WORSE badge had no test.
- **Round 4:**
  - The same "Act:" loss came back when the vuln's site also carried an unchanged Broken item. `orderAttention` keeps a site's items together, so its worst-ranked site came first. The candidates are now ranked on their own.
  - Three lines no test pinned. The badge rule moved into a pure function to test them directly.
- **Round 5:** clean on all three lenses.

**Measured.** 19 mutants, all red at `f0ba1e71`: the brief's 6 (its third split into 3a and 3b), plus one per review round's defect.

The operator lens re-rendered the email over 14 nights (failed deploy, exhausted vuln, red CI, day-one Renovate PR):

- night 0 reads `Act: Sonder — latest production deploy error (+1 more)`;
- the vuln's rise on night 2 is WORSE;
- Beta's CI recovering on night 3 skips cleanly;
- night 7 mails the Renovate PR as `1 new`;

**Beliefs corrected on contact.**

- **"The snapshot's badge is the email's badge."** It is not. The snapshot also holds the Watch days no mail carried, so the first mail of a risen Renovate PR read "standing".
- **"`orderAttention` puts the worst item first."** It puts the worst _site_ first.
