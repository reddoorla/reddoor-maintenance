**The window.** Classes, widths and offsets were each standing in for the real question: is this visible once the page has settled? The JS render now asks it directly. After it settles, it samples every 500 ms for 6 s, and an item counts as visible with JavaScript only if every sample shows it. A slide that autoplay brings on screen, whether it slides, fades or varies in width, is absent from some samples and is not judged. The note counts such items. The parked rule and its geometry are gone, and so are their mutation rows.

The cost is about 6 s more per width. The tests use a 2.5 s window, which every slideshow fixture cycles through. The negative control uses the default.

- **Must fail:** the two columns and the section moved by its width.
- **Still held:** round 4's wrapper slide-in and active-class carousel.

**Review round 6 was dirty, so R4 is parked** (the operator's answer in workspace `decisions/2026-10-21-0105-od18-r4-round-five-dirty.md`: park if the sixth round is dirty). The cut lens was clean. The two behaviour lenses reproduced these, each with a fixture in the session's scratchpad:

- **Window false passes.**
  - Anything that changes after the settle is read from the settle sample, so its final state is never compared. Three cases:
    - a count-up that finishes during the window (0 to 1,200 over 8 s, "0" served);
    - a typewriter headline that finishes typing during the window;
    - copy, a reveal or a lazy image that appears during the window.
  - The fix the lenses propose is to judge each item's final value once it stopped changing, and to take newly steady items from the end of the window.
- **A window false fail.** An autoplay slower than the window (7–10 s) holds one slide still for the whole window, which then counts as steady. The verdict depends on how long the reveal pass took. Round 5 admitted "an autoplay slower than the window", but at 7–10 s, which is common, it is a realistic false fail.
- **A false pass older than the window.** The clip test treats any `overflow` or `clip-path` ancestor as clipping, whatever the containing block. An absolutely positioned child of a `position: static; overflow: hidden; height: 0` stage is drawn outside the stage, yet reads as clipped. On the vida past page at 390 px, that is why the hero's label and `<h1>` were never judged there: they fail at 1440 px only. The fix is to count a clipper only when it is the element's containing block or an ancestor of it.
- **Unheld.** The note's unsteady count has no test.
