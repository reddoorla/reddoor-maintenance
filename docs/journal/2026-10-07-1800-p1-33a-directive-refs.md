**Review cost, honestly: six rounds, the most of any PR this week.** Each round past the second went to the operator, who chose a further round four times. Every round found a real false PASS, and every one came from the same mistake: the reader accepted text it did not fully understand.

- **Rounds 1–3: shapes added one at a time.** Each fix added a citation shape to read (unquoted names, curly quotes, links, emphasis), and each new shape left a neighbour silently uncounted. A dead heading written in that neighbour passed. Round 2's false PASS was a regression caused by round 1's fix.
- **Round 3's answer: invert the default.** Any `CLAUDE.md` or `AUTONOMY.md` code span followed by → or § is a citation, and anything the reader cannot read fails as "cannot read this citation".
- **Round 4: the unquoted shape cannot know where it ends.** Two real headings contain a comma. `→ Before a fleet sweep, check the archive list` was checked only up to the comma, so a heading that does not exist resolved. Quotes became required.
- **Rounds 5 and 6: the same cut, at a quote inside the heading.** `AUTONOMY.md` has `## Merge authority (current policy: "everything but releases")`. The reader stopped at the first inner quote, so if the policy were renamed, the old full citation would still resolve.
  - The round-5 fix refused a closing quote followed by a letter. Round 6 showed that a space, punctuation or an accented letter after the inner quote still got through.
  - Round 6's fix lists what may follow a closing quote (whitespace, `, . ; : ) ] ! ? — –`, or the end of the paragraph) and refuses quoted text that begins or ends with a space. Writing it, I found a seventh hole the reviewer had not tried: `"The ("--force") flag"` read as "The (". The list closes that too.

The lesson repeated four times: refusing what the reader cannot parse closed a whole class at once, and adding what it could parse never did. The cost is accepted and written in the test's header: a valid citation in an unusual spelling fails loudly, and the message gives the spelling to use.
