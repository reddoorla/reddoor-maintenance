# One post, every channel: a social post kit started by a person

For Tim, for Monday's conversation (2026-10-05). Follows the #rd-marketing thread from Friday.

## The idea in two lines

When one of us has a reason to post, we hand an agent a project and a one-line seed (why this one, why now). It comes back with a finished **post kit**: a caption written for each channel, plus images cropped for each channel. You approve or edit it, then publish it to Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn **from one place**, either right away or scheduled.

Nothing runs on a timer and nothing posts without a person. **The target is under an hour from "we should post this" to "it's live everywhere and looks right."**

## What's in a kit

You start one with a project and a seed, for example "Progress Lighting, because the ads this month are about print." The kit comes back with:

- **A caption for each channel**, all from the same problem/solution/outcome material, written in sales language you approve once:
  - **Instagram:** short, visual first, "link in bio";
  - **Facebook:** the Instagram caption with a real link;
  - **LinkedIn:** longer, in the firm's voice, linking straight to the project page and without a wall of hashtags.
- **2–3 images** from the project's portfolio page, cropped for every format:
  - 1080×1350 for the Instagram and Facebook feeds;
  - 1080×1080 squares, which work everywhere, LinkedIn included;
  - 1080×1920 for stories.

  Each crop is checked by eye. Where cropping would cut off type or a cover, the image is set on white instead.

- **Alt text** for each image.
- **Tracked links**, so Google Analytics shows which channel sent each visit to reddoorla.com.

The kit never makes up an outcome. If the portfolio page doesn't state a result, the caption reads **[outcome: Tim to supply]** and you fill it in.

A lucky find makes this cheaper than it sounds. Every project linked from reddoorla.com/portfolio already has a **"The Challenge"** block and an **"Our Solution"** block with three titled points. Our own site already answers problem/solution/outcome. The kit mostly reorganizes and tightens what's there.

## Posting from one place

Reddoor's site links to Instagram and LinkedIn. Your Meta ads come with a Facebook Page, so that's three channels.

- **Recommended: a social scheduler** such as Buffer, Later or Hootsuite. You connect the three accounts to it once. After that, one composer takes a separate caption per channel and posts to all of them now or at a set time. These tools are already approved by Meta and LinkedIn, so we skip the app reviews. Most have a free or low-cost tier; we'd check current pricing before picking one.
- **Free, but two places:** Meta Business Suite covers Instagram and Facebook together, and LinkedIn's own composer schedules company-page posts. It works and costs nothing, but it adds about five minutes per post.
- **Not recommended: our own posting robot.** We would need a Meta business app and app review, plus LinkedIn's approval for posting as a company. That's weeks of setup, mostly waiting on them, to save a few minutes per post over a scheduler.

## How big is it, honestly

As I said Friday, "good" costs more than "working." These estimates assume good.

- **An afternoon:** pick the scheduler and connect the three accounts; build the first one or two kits by hand with an agent; write the checklist and prompt that make the next kit come out the same way.
- **A few days (2–4):** a repeatable kit. You (or I) run one command with a project and a seed, and a few minutes later the finished kit is in your email. If the scheduler we pick can accept drafts from outside (to verify before we promise it), the kit lands there as a ready-made draft, so your part shrinks to reading, editing and pressing schedule. Most of the time goes to getting the voice right for three channels, not to the plumbing.
- **Optional, a day or two of design:** a light branded frame for images that need one, so every post reads as ours at a glance. This is where "looks beautiful" stops being luck.
- **Weeks, and not recommended:** our own posting robot, as above.

## Who does what

**You, each time you want a post (30–40 minutes, most of it editing):** name the project and the seed, edit the captions until they sound right, and publish from the scheduler. Choosing the seed is the intention part; the kit takes the production off your plate.

**Once, up front:**

- You approve the voice: a few lines on how we sound, with one caption you like for Instagram and one for LinkedIn.
- You connect Instagram, the Facebook Page and the LinkedIn company page to the scheduler. You need admin access on each for that.
- We pick five projects to start from. My suggestion is Progress Lighting, Rubrik Zero Labs, Texas Organ Sharing Alliance, Gallery Sonder and 1-800-DENTIST.
- We decide whether only the 12 projects linked from /portfolio count, or whether the 40 or so older ones in the CMS are fair game too.
- About half the public pages state a concrete result (awards, traffic, a client quote). The rest describe what we made. For those, you supply the outcome line once and it's reused.

One example of why the approval step matters: the 1-800-DENTIST page says "15x Growth in Web Traffic" in one place, and "from hundreds to 773,000 unique visitors over the last twelve months" in another. Those don't describe the same number, so that page needs a correction before it goes out anywhere.

## How we'll know in 8 weeks

Your Meta ads should send people to the profiles, so we watch:

- Instagram profile visits and LinkedIn page visitors;
- visits to reddoorla.com from each channel, which the tracked links make visible;
- inbound DMs and messages that turn into a real conversation.

We don't watch follower count. If the posts we make over eight weeks don't move any of those, we stop, and we've spent a few days. That also gets at my question from Friday: has a client ever found us through social?

## Separately: "systems to help me do the maintaining"

This is a different conversation, but the smallest useful version is small. Every Monday morning you'd get one email listing your recurring tasks for the week (follow-ups and anything else you name), with last week's open items carried over. No new app to learn. If it helps after a month, we turn it into a real task system. Making the list of recurring tasks is the work, so that's where we'd start.
